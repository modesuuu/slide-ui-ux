Problem and Background

Problem & Background

Three points from our research.

Problem Statement

Users struggle to find collaborators who fit a project's needs, especially in skill, role, and interest. They have ended up with members whose skills did not match, and some roles are simply hard to fill.

Current Situation

  • Asking friends
  • Posting in group chats
  • Scrolling communities
  • Messaging people on social media

Impact

The cost is time lost before the work even begins, and teams that start with the wrong people.

  • Skill
  • Role
  • Interest
  • Asking friends
  • Posting in group chats
  • Scrolling communities
  • Messaging people on social media

Based on a questionnaire survey of 20 students across faculties.

Target Users and User Needs

From our research

Target Users & User Needs

Target users

Students · School students · Teachers · Software Engineers · IT Practitioners

user needs

Partners who fit the project: the right skill, the right role, a real interest in the field · Information you can see before deciding: what a person can do, and what a project needs · One central place to search

Pain point

The search is scattered across friends, group chats, communities, and social media · Skills stay invisible until the work has started · Finding a collaborator takes time and effort

Research Methodology

We didn’t assume. We asked.

Solution & Key Features · 06

Main feature.

Proposed solution

Underflow’s main feature is Collaborations: students post a project, or join one, and do real project work with a real team. Every posting shows what the project needs, and every profile shows what a person can do. What the team ships becomes their portfolio

Collaborations turn group work into proof: not just a grade, but a portfolio. The supporting features answer the needs you saw in chapter three. Nothing here is decoration.

User value
